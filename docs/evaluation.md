# Evaluation

Generated by `python -m wl.evaluate` on 12 synthetic reserves (seeds 300 to 311; 60 cameras, 16 microphones, 30 days each) that the demo and the tests never use.
There are no photographs or recordings: a camera detection is a label with a confidence, a sound is five summary features. Read these as a check that the methods work, not as field accuracy.

## 1. Which sites does each species use?

Six species, two 15-day periods, 12 reserves. Error is the absolute difference from the true share of camera sites used.

| | Mean absolute error |
|---|---|
| Any detection of the species at the site, at any confidence | 0.440 |
| Any detection at confidence 0.9 or more | 0.040 |
| Occupancy model on those confident detections | 0.048 |

For the two hard-to-see species (leopard, hyena; detected on at most a fifth of days where present) the confident count is off by -0.067 on average and the occupancy model by +0.061.
Counting every low-confidence label puts almost every species almost everywhere: the confidence threshold is what matters most. For shy species the confident count misses sites where the animal was never caught;
the occupancy model corrects in the right direction and overshoots by about as much, because one confident mislabel at an unused site counts as presence. On average it is no better than the plain count here.

## 2. Corridor shift

The herd's crossing point moves 6.5 km east on day 15. Estimated from confident camera detections alone: 5.26 km on average (range 3.22 to 6.41); permutation p below 0.01 in 12 of 12 reserves.
The same test on antelope, which do not migrate, gave p below 0.01 in 2 of 12. Antelope do lose sites in the cleared block, so some real movement is expected there.

## 3. Night vehicles

Each reserve has one planted pattern: vehicles on six nights at three cameras on the boundary track. Found in 12 of 12 reserves, with 0 other clusters raised.
For scale: 28 cameras per reserve recorded at least one night-time "vehicle" label at some confidence; alerting on each would be 28 sites to visit instead of one.

## 4. Gunshots

Sound classifier: 96.6% accuracy over five classes on 30,000 held-out sounds. On that balanced test set a 0.5 threshold looks excellent (91.9% precision),
but a gunshot is about one sound in a thousand in the field, where the same threshold would be right 4.3% of the time. The alert threshold (0.935) is the lowest at which a quarter of alerts are expected to be real at that prevalence; it keeps 57.7% of gunshots.

| Across the reserves | Alerts raised | True gunshots among them | True gunshots in the data |
|---|---|---|---|
| Classifier at the chosen threshold | 47 | 16 | 36 |
| Baseline: short and loud | 3750 | 35 | 36 |

Gunshots are 36 of about 32,400 sounds. The strict threshold trades missed gunshots for alerts a ranger can afford to act on, and an alert is still evidence for a person to weigh, cross-checked against cameras.

## 5. Patrols

Share of mapped risk within 1.5 km of a patrol path, three teams, eight hours each:

| | Risk covered |
|---|---|
| Prize-collecting routes | 90.2% |
| Baseline: each team follows a stretch of the boundary track | 74.8% |

The risk map is built from the same alerts the routes are then scored on, and its weights are a judgement, so this measures the router, not whether patrols prevent poaching.
